# FingerWorks demo video — script outline

**Premise:** A lab researcher is mid-protocol with gloved/contaminated hands — cannot
touch the mouse or keyboard — but needs to check a message, pull up a reference image,
and log a quick note. One continuous, believable task, ~75–90 seconds, every feature
earns its place by actually being needed in the moment rather than being listed.

**Framing:** Screen recording (cursor + overlay clearly visible) with a small
picture-in-picture of the researcher's hand/gestures (or the `--debug` live camera
window, which already shows the skeleton overlay — genuinely useful here, not just
a debug tool). Captions burned in for each gesture the first time it's used.

---

## 0:00–0:08 — Hook

Cold open, no narration yet. Researcher at the bench, gloves visibly on, one hand
raises toward a webcam clipped to the monitor. The cursor moves on screen with **no
mouse in frame at all**.

> **On-screen text:** "Gloves on. Hands full. Still working."

This is the whole pitch in one shot — don't explain it yet, just show it.

## 0:08–0:15 — Setup line

One line of voiceover/caption, then straight into the task:

> "A message just came in. Let's answer it — without touching anything."

## 0:15–0:45 — The task (feature walkthrough)

Each beat is something the task actually needs, not a gesture demo for its own sake.

1. **Cursor move + left-click** — hand moves, cursor tracks it; a chat notification
   sits in the corner. Pinch thumb+index to click it open. *(First gesture callout:
   "Move hand: cursor · Pinch: click" — green ring flashes at the cursor on the
   click, quick visual payoff.)*
2. **Scroll to read** — the message is a longer thread. Point index finger up with
   the **left** hand to scroll up through it, thumb-down to scroll back. Smooth,
   continuous — this is the moment to show it doesn't feel choppy.
3. **Zoom in** — thread references a gel image thumbnail, too small to read. Open
   left hand (all five fingers) to zoom the whole screen in on it; closed fist to
   zoom back out once it's confirmed. This sells the "precision without touching"
   angle hard — zoom, not scroll-to-resize.
4. **Right-click** — right-click the image to copy it (pinch thumb+ring). Quick,
   almost throwaway — shows the second click type exists without dwelling on it.
5. **Keyboard toggle** — closed right-hand fist brings up the on-screen keyboard.
   This is the "oh, it really is a full replacement" beat.
6. **Typing the reply** — type a short, real reply (not gibberish) using the
   keyboard: a capital letter (Shift), a symbol via the `123` page (e.g. a `%` for
   a concentration), a backspace correction. Show the panel/keyboard being
   **dragged** out of the way of the content it was covering mid-type — this is a
   good spot for the newest feature, motivated by an actual need instead of shown
   in isolation.
7. **Paste** — left-hand scissors (index+middle) pastes the copied image reference
   straight into the reply, no keyboard needed for it.
8. **Cut typed text** — right-hand scissors on a line they typed by mistake, shown
   as a quick "oops, undo that" beat rather than a dry feature list item.
9. **Send / close keyboard** — fist again to close the keyboard, pinch to hit send.

## 0:45–0:55 — The pause beat

Someone calls the researcher's name from off-frame. Thumb+pinky pose — tracking
visibly pauses (control bar dot goes orange, "Paused"). They step away and come back;
click **Resume** on the control bar (proving the control bar itself is reachable
without a gesture, for exactly this reason) rather than needing to re-pose.

> **Caption:** "Step away without losing your place."

## 0:55–1:10 — The small stuff, fast

A quick, upbeat montage — 2–3 seconds each, no narration needed, just captions:

- Settings window opens (pinch on **Settings**) — cursor snappiness slider dragged
  live, cursor visibly gets snappier. *("Tune it to your hand.")*
- Click indicator flashing green/yellow on a couple of clicks in quick succession.
  *("Always know it landed.")*
- Control bar dragged to a different corner mid-task. *("Move it out of your way.")*

## 1:10–1:20 — Close

Cut back to a wide shot: researcher's hands stay exactly where they were (still
gloved, still at the bench), task done, screen shows the sent reply.

> **Voiceover:** "Same hands. Same gloves. Never touched a thing."
> **On-screen text / end card:** FingerWorks — logo/name, one-line tagline,
> where to get it.

---

## Notes for whoever shoots this

- **Don't narrate every gesture out loud** — captions for the first instance of each
  gesture are enough; a voice reading a feature list over each click gets tedious
  fast. Save spoken narration for the hook and the close.
- **Keep `--debug`'s live camera window on screen** (small, corner) for the whole
  demo if it doesn't clutter the shot — the hand-skeleton overlay is the clearest
  possible proof this is really gesture-driven, not a mouse just kept off-camera.
- **One continuous take if possible** (or invisible cuts) — the moment the video
  cuts away from the hands, it stops proving anything.
- **Real content, not lorem ipsum** — the chat thread, the image, the typed
  reply should all look like an actual lab workflow; specificity is what makes
  the "believable task" framing land instead of reading as a feature tour.
